Detailed guide

What you are going to learn

1

Patching your moving heads in MyStrow

The first step is declaring your moving heads in MyStrow's DMX patch. Pick the "Moving Head" fixture type, select the channel count (16CH, 17CH mode, or whatever your manual says), set the start DMX address and assign it to a group. MyStrow works out the addressing of every channel automatically.

2

Setting the movement limits

Every moving head has mechanical end stops in pan (horizontal rotation) and tilt. In MyStrow you set the min/max limits of each axis so the fixture never leaves the useful area of your stage. Those limits are respected automatically by every programmed effect and movement.

3

The shutter and the dimmer

The shutter is the mechanism that opens and closes the beam. In MyStrow it is mapped straight onto the right channel of your fixture's profile. The dimmer (intensity) and the strobe are also reachable from the interface, without you having to know the exact channel number.

4

2D stage plot and groups

Once your moving heads are patched, they appear in the interactive stage plot. You can click a fixture to select it, drag to aim its beam, and organise your machines into groups to control them collectively. The stage plot reflects the position of the beams on stage in real time.

5

Color wheel or RGB: picking the right mode

Some moving heads have a color wheel with fixed filters, others use RGB or CMY mixing. MyStrow detects your fixture's color type and adapts the interface: a wheel-position selector for color wheels, a free color picker for RGB/CMY. The keyboard shortcuts (W = white, R = red…) work in both modes.

6

Hands-on and 3D view

MyStrow includes a 3D view of your room that renders your moving heads' beams in real time. Orbit the camera to check the beam angles from any viewpoint, and use the camera presets (front of house, top view, side) to move around quickly. The 3D view updates at the same pace as your moves on the 2D plot.

7

Automatic symmetry

MyStrow's SYM mode lets you control two symmetrical moving heads with a single gesture: when you move one fixture's pan, its mirror automatically goes the opposite way. Ideal for V setups, crossed "X" looks or beam-convergence effects, without programming each fixture by hand.

8

Recording a sequence

With the Light REC button you record your actions live: moving head movements, color changes, shutter openings… MyStrow turns those actions into keyframes on the timeline. You can then edit, move and fine-tune each keyframe to build a sequence perfectly synced to your music.

9

Creating dynamic effects

MyStrow's effects engine generates automatic movements: circles, figure eights, horizontal/vertical sweeps, random moves. Every effect is adjustable (speed, amplitude, movement centre) and applies in one action to all your selected fixtures. No line-by-line programming — a few clicks are enough for a professional show.

Frequently asked questions

DMX moving heads with MyStrow

Which moving heads are compatible with MyStrow?

Any standard DMX moving head is compatible. MyStrow handles 8-bit and 16-bit profiles for pan/tilt, color wheels, gobos, dimmers and shutters. If your fixture speaks DMX (the universal standard protocol), MyStrow controls it — whether it is a Eurolite, Chauvet, Cameo, Showtec or a professional brand.

How do I handle several moving heads with different profiles?

Every fixture can have its own profile in the MyStrow patch. You can mix 12CH and 16CH moving heads in the same show, on different addresses. MyStrow normalises the commands so you can control them collectively from the same interface, whatever channel mode each machine uses.

Does symmetry work with more than 2 moving heads?

Yes. MyStrow applies pan symmetry across the left/right halves of your selection, whatever the number of fixtures. With 4 fixtures selected, the first 2 go one way and the next 2 the other — perfect for multi-fixture setups on a lighting bar.

Can I combine dynamic effects and recorded sequences?

Absolutely. Start a dynamic effect on your moving heads, then hit Light REC: MyStrow records the movement into the timeline in real time. You get a smooth, repeatable sequence that you can then edit keyframe by keyframe to refine it.
