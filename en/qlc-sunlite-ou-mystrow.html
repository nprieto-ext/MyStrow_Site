The real question

Programming, or playing?

Let's say it up front: QLC+ and Sunlite are good programs. They have been driving DMX for years and thousands of people use them every weekend. This comparison isn't here to knock them down.

The real difference isn't “which one is best” but who each one is for. QLC+ and Sunlite are programming tools: you build your show scene by scene, step by step, then you play it. MyStrow goes the other way: you plug in and play, and you program only if you want to.

Depending on whether you enjoy programming or just want things to work on the night, the right answer changes completely.

QLC+

Free, complete… and to be programmed

QLC+ (Q Light Controller Plus) is free, open-source software that runs on Windows, Mac, Linux and even Raspberry Pi. It is very complete: scenes, chasers, RGB matrices, a virtual console, sound triggers, browser access, and a 3D view in version 5.

Its logic is that of a lighting programmer. You patch your fixtures, then create every scene, every chaser and every function yourself. On the MIDI side, QLC+ knows many controllers, but it's up to you to assign every button and fader to a function of the virtual console.

  • Pros: free, unlimited, cross-platform, a large community.
  • Cons: everything is built by hand. Expect several evenings before you have a playable show if you start from scratch.
  • Best for: people with time, who like to understand and tweak every detail, on a zero software budget.
Sunlite

The software that comes with its interface

Sunlite, published by Nicolaudie, is a well-established name in DMX. The software comes with the brand's interfaces, which makes it a popular all-in-one purchase. It has a 3D view and a large fixture library.

It also relies on a programming approach, by scenes and steps. Sunlite Suite 3 is Windows software: on Mac, you need other programs from the Nicolaudie range.

One important thing to know before buying: Nicolaudie interfaces are locked to the brand's own software. Neither MyStrow nor QLC+ can drive them. If you ever switch software, the interface won't follow you. We checked it with the Sushi-Z1.

  • Pros: software and interface bought together, a proven tool, 3D view.
  • Cons: step-by-step programming, Windows for Sunlite Suite 3, interface tied to the brand.
  • Best for: people who already own a Sunlite interface, work on a PC and prepare their shows in advance.

FAQ

Questions we often get

Is QLC+ really free?

Yes. QLC+ is free, open-source software with no time or feature limit. You only need to buy a compatible DMX interface. What you don't pay in money, you pay in programming time.

Does my Sunlite interface work with MyStrow?

Generally not. Nicolaudie interfaces (Sunlite, Daslight, Sushi-Z1) are locked to the brand's own software: neither MyStrow nor QLC+ can drive them. MyStrow works with open USB-DMX interfaces (ENTTEC-style) and Art-Net nodes.

I already know QLC+. Will I find my way around MyStrow?

Yes: patch, groups, memories, effects — the concepts are the same. You'll mostly have less to do, since the controller and the basic effects are ready to use. The beginner guide walks you through it in about fifteen minutes.

Does MyStrow run on Mac?

Yes, on Windows and Mac. QLC+ also does (and on Linux). Sunlite Suite 3 is Windows software: on Mac, Nicolaudie offers other programs from its range. On Mac, MyStrow needs a “PRO” USB-DMX interface or an Art-Net node.

Which DMX software is best for a DJ who is new to lighting?

If you want a result on the night without programming anything, MyStrow is built for that: the AKAI APC mini is recognised automatically and LIVE mode follows the music. If you have time and zero budget, QLC+ will do the job — as long as you program it.
