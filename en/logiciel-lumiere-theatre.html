The starting point

What a theatre lighting desk needs

In theatre, the lighting doesn't follow a tempo: it follows the script and the staging. Dawn breaks, a door opens, a character is left alone in a profile spot… Each change is a lighting state, triggered on the right line, with a shorter or longer fade.

So a theatre lighting desk needs four things:

  • A cue list: the lighting states, in the order of the show.
  • Timed fades: moving from one state to the next in 3, 8 or 20 seconds, smoothly.
  • A lighting plot: knowing which fixture lights what, and which DMX address it answers to.
  • Sound: music and sound effects, often run by the same person as the lighting.

Theatre lighting consoles do all of this very well, but they are expensive and take time to learn. For a group that plays a few dates a year, MyStrow does the same job from a computer, with simpler wording and a simpler interface.

Step by step

Building your lighting cue list with MyStrow

1

Draw the lighting plot

Place your fixtures on the 3D stage plot: front light, backlights, side lights, profiles. Give them their DMX address and sort them into groups. The plot shows the beams in real time, so you can prepare everything without being in the venue.

2

Create your lighting states

Set the look of a scene (intensities, colours, moving head positions if you have any) and save it to a memory. A memory is a complete lighting state that you recall in one move.

3

Set the fades

Each memory has its own fade time: 0 for a hard cut (a sudden blackout, a thunderclap), a few seconds for a sunrise. A memory can also hold a sequence of cues, each with its own fade: every press takes the lighting to the next cue.

4

Add the sound

Walk-in music, sound effects and videos go into the playlist, in the same place as the lighting. A PAUSE line waits for your go before moving on, and tracks can end with a fade-out.

5

Rehearse, then run the show

On the night, running the show comes down to pressing at the right moment: on a pad, a keyboard key or from a tablet at the back of the hall. The whole show is saved in a single file that you can take to another computer.

FAQ

Questions we often get

Can I prepare the lighting cues without being in the venue?

Yes. MyStrow's 3D lighting plot shows your fixtures, their beams and colours just as on stage: you build your states and fades at home, then plug in the DMX interface on the day of the dress rehearsal.

Can an inexperienced volunteer run the lighting desk?

Yes, that's the idea. Once the cue list is ready, running the show comes down to pressing the right pad or key at the right moment. Sound and music sit in the same list as the lighting, so one person can run both.

Are my halogen fixtures (fresnels, profiles, PAR 64) compatible?

Yes, as long as they are plugged into a DMX dimmer pack. MyStrow drives the dimmer like any other DMX device, alongside your LED fixtures.
