Why a separate category

A laser is not like other lights

On a DMX laser, channel 1 often does two things: it switches the unit off and selects its mode (auto, sound or DMX). Treated as a plain dimmer, it would flip the laser into sound mode in the middle of your show.

Many lasers also hide an auto program in the upper part of their channels: from 0 to 127 you place the pattern; above 128, it starts moving on its own.

That's why MyStrow has a real “Laser” category, available since version 3.2.6. It takes these quirks into account, and your laser also gets everything that already exists for moving heads.

Step 1

Add your laser to the patch

1

Search the library

Several lasers are already described, for example the Laserworld CS-1000RGB MKII, the Mac Mah MAC 1000 RGB (16 and 36 channels), the generic Laser Show System 10 channels (sold under several brands) and the Aworldlaser series. Type the brand or model in the fixture library.

2

Or import its file

A QLC+, GDTF or GrandMA file of your laser is recognised as a laser on import. See how to import a fixture.

3

Or start from a generic profile

In the DMX Patch, choose the Laser type: two profiles, 5 channels and 8 channels, cover entry-level lasers. Just check the channel order in the manual.

Step 2

Control it from the lighting plot

Right-click your laser in the lighting plot (or several selected lasers). A dedicated menu opens:

  • LASER row: Off (the laser emits nothing), DMX (MyStrow controls it) or Auto / Sound (the laser runs on its own).
  • Automatic wake-up: if the laser is off and you touch a setting, it switches to DMX by itself.
  • Pattern: the ◀ ▶ arrows step through the drawings one by one (“Pattern 12 / 51”), no need to hunt for the right DMX value.
  • ANIMATION: rotation, wave, pattern size, progressive drawing… depending on what your laser offers.

Why is my laser off at startup? That's on purpose: at rest, channel 1 is 0, so the laser stays off. One click on DMX in the right-click menu (or any setting) is enough to turn it on. So there's no risk of a laser shot when the show opens.

Moving the pattern goes through Pan and Tilt, like a moving head. Your positions, the REC Light Position track, movement effects (circle, figure eight, sweep) and the gamepad stick therefore work directly on the laser. MyStrow knows a laser sweeps a much smaller angle than a moving head: your circles stay round.

The trick that changes everything

“Usable range”: no more auto movements

On a laser whose position channels switch to auto above 128, just cap the channel: right-click the channel in the patch, then Usable range, and pick for example 0-127.

MyStrow then scales the whole fader travel into that range. Your fader stays usable end to end, but it never sends a value that would trigger the internal program. Built-in profiles already have this setting when the manual calls for it.

Useful for other devices too

Any device that starts an auto program in the upper part of a channel can be capped the same way: entry-level lights, effects, smoke machines.

Safety

A few rules before switching on

  • Never in the eyes: never aim a laser at the audience or at a person, not even for a moment.
  • Follow the regulations: high-power lasers are strictly regulated. Check with the venue or organiser before any public show.
  • Keep a cut-off within reach: the unit's key switch or emergency stop comes before any software.
  • Check the ranges in your manual: some values of the command channel switch off, reset or start a program. MyStrow warns you when you animate a channel of this kind.

Good to know: in the 3D plot, a laser is shown as a beam, like a moving head. Its drawings are not simulated in 3D yet: to see the real pattern, look at the laser itself.
